A cooling towel has an unusual care problem. Ordinary towels dry out between
uses; a cooling towel is designed to stay damp, and damp fabric in a warm dark
bag is how mildew starts. Most cooling towels are not worn out when they are
thrown away — they are thrown away because they smell.

## The daily routine

**Rinse in clean water after every use.** Sweat, sunscreen and dust all end up
in the fabric, and they are what the mould feeds on. Thirty seconds under a tap
does it. This is the single highest-value habit on this page.

**Wring it out properly.** Not for cooling this time, but so it is not carrying
surplus water into storage.

**Then pick one storage state and stick to it.**

- *Dry and open.* Hang it until it is completely dry, then store it loose. For
  PVA this means it will go rigid; that is normal and it revives under a tap in
  thirty seconds.
- *Damp and sealed.* Store it damp in a genuinely airtight container — the tube
  most PVA towels ship in is designed for this. Good for a few days, keeps the
  towel soft and instantly usable, and no substitute for the rinse.

What kills towels is neither of these: damp, folded, in a kit bag, until next
week.

## Deeper cleaning

### Microfibre

Machine wash on a cool or warm cycle. No fabric softener — it leaves a coating
that reduces the fabric's ability to hold and release water, which is the whole
point of the product. A splash of white vinegar in the rinse instead does the
job and helps with odour. Air dry; a hot tumble dryer damages microfibre.

### PVA

Hand wash. A machine's agitation and spin cycle can tear the open-cell
structure, and manufacturers generally advise against it. Warm water, a little
mild detergent, squeeze it through gently rather than wringing or scrubbing,
then rinse until the water runs clear. Never use bleach: it degrades the
polymer.

## Rescuing one that already smells

1. **Soak in a vinegar solution.** One part white vinegar to four parts warm
   water, for an hour. Vinegar's acidity is effective against the organisms
   causing the odour and it will not damage either material.
2. **Rinse thoroughly.** Until there is no vinegar smell left.
3. **If it persists, repeat with a longer soak.** Overnight is fine.
4. **Dry completely, in sunlight if you can.** UV is a genuine help here, and
   drying fully is what breaks the cycle.

If two rounds of this do not fix it, the mould is established deep in the
structure and the towel is finished. That is worth knowing early rather than
carrying a smelly towel around for another month.

## What not to do

**No fabric softener, ever.** It coats fibres and reduces water handling on
both materials.

**No bleach on PVA.** It attacks the polymer.

**No hot tumble drying.** Heat damages microfibre and makes PVA brittle.

**Do not leave a PVA towel in direct sun to dry for days on end.** Drying it
fully is good; prolonged UV exposure over weeks will eventually make it
crumbly.

## The mildew point is worth repeating

The reason this page exists is that "my cooling towel smells" is one of the
most common complaints in the category, and it is almost always a storage
problem rather than a product problem. Rinse it, wring it, and pick a storage
state. That is the whole routine.
