## The position

The merchant sells an evaporative cooling towel. Evaporative cooling is real,
measurable physics — which is unusual in this corner of the market and is the
whole opportunity.

Most competitors sell on an unfalsifiable number ("12 degrees cooler for 3
hours") with no stated conditions. We explain the mechanism, state its limits,
and let the reader predict what they will get. That is slower than hype and it
is the only durable position available to a site with no domain authority.

**One rule above all others: never call a PVA or microfibre towel organic.**
They are engineered synthetics. The organic creams arriving later are a
different product line with different claims, and the credibility of that line
depends on not having borrowed its halo for the towels.

## Timing: the seasonality problem, stated plainly

Cooling towels peak in June–August in the UK and US. The site cannot launch
until late September, which is the end of the northern season.

**This is not the problem it looks like, but only if the winter is used
properly.** A new domain takes roughly three to six months to rank for anything
competitive. Launching in late September and treating October to March as the
ranking ramp puts the site in position for **May–August 2027**, which is the
first season it could realistically have won anyway. Launching in June would
have meant competing at full season strength from zero authority.

What that means concretely:

- **Oct–Dec:** publish, build internal linking, get indexed. Expect almost no
  traffic and do not read anything into that.
- **Jan–Mar:** the heat-safety cluster starts to mature. Southern-hemisphere
  traffic (Australia, South Africa) is *in season* — worth a dedicated push,
  and worth remembering that our dual-currency setup already serves them badly
  unless AUD is added.
- **Apr–May:** the ramp. This is when to have everything ready, not when to
  start writing.
- **Jun–Aug 2027:** the season the site is actually built for.

The equine and skincare lines are the hedge. **Mud fever and sweet itch peak in
autumn and winter** — precisely the months cooling is dead. Publishing the
equine cluster over winter is not filler; it is what makes the site a
year-round business rather than a three-month one.

## Site structure

Three pillars at launch. Every guide declares one in frontmatter, which
generates the two-way link between hub and post automatically.

| Pillar | URL | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Cooling towels | `/cooling-towels` | The money pillar. Product intent. |
| Working safely in heat | `/heat-safety` | The traffic pillar. No product intent, highest trust. |
| Organic skin & body | `/skin` | Pre-seeded for the coming range. |

**An equine pillar was built and then removed** at the operator's request,
because the product information is not confirmed yet. The architecture still
supports it — see `CLAUDE.md`, "Adding a product category". The equine guide
cluster stays in the plan below, because it is the right *seasonal* hedge and
that reasoning does not expire.

**Why heat safety is a pillar and not a blog post.** It is the highest-intent
traffic on the site and it is not a shopping query. "Heat exhaustion vs heat
stroke" is searched by someone who needs to act now, on a phone, outdoors. That
audience is exactly our buyer, met at the moment they trust a page most. It
also earns links, which product pages never do.

## The lesson that decides what we write

**Ranking first is not the same as getting clicks.**

On the sister site, `/careers/salary` ranks **position 1** for roughly
seventeen "how much do X make" queries and earns **zero clicks from any of
them**, because Google answers salary questions in the SERP with an AI Overview
or a pay widget. You cannot rank higher than first, and no title, schema or
content change recovers those.

Before writing anything, ask: **can Google answer this without a click?** If
yes, better rankings buy nothing.

Applied here:

| Avoid | Because | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "What is a cooling towel" | Answered in the SERP | "PVA vs microfibre — which should you buy" |
| "How does evaporation work" | Answered in the SERP | "Why yours barely works in humid heat" |
| "How much do cooling towels cost" | Price widget | "Cooling towels compared" (table) |
| "Normal horse temperature" | Featured snippet | "How to cool a hot horse properly" |

The shapes that require a click: **comparisons, procedures, checklists,
calculators, templates, and "why is mine not working"**. On the sister site
those convert at 6–10% against a 0.57% site average. Every guide published so
far is one of those shapes, deliberately.

## Google Ads

**Do not run ads before the site is ranking, and probably not for a while
after.** An affiliate site paying for clicks on "cooling towel" competes with
the merchants themselves, who can afford a much higher cost per click because
they keep the whole margin rather than a commission on it. That maths does not
work.

If ads are run, three rules:

1. **Never bid on the merchant's brand terms.** Most affiliate programmes
   prohibit it outright and it is grounds for termination.
2. **Advertise the tools, not the products.** `/tools/heat-index` is cheap to
   promote, useful enough to earn a bookmark, and builds an audience that
   returns in season.
3. **Expect claim scrutiny.** Google's healthcare and medicines policy requires
   efficacy claims to be substantiable, and heat-illness content sits close
   enough to that line to attract review. Keep ad copy to mechanism and
   comparison, never "prevents heat stroke".

Better use of the same money: pay for one round of genuine product photography
and a professional shoot of the wet-wring-snap sequence for social.

## Social: TikTok, Instagram, Facebook

The handle is `@cleanlivinghq` on all three. **Claim all three before launch
even if nothing is posted** — a squatted handle is expensive to undo.

### Disclosure is not optional

The ASA has ruled that a link or discount code on its own does **not** make a
post's commercial nature clear. Every post with a commercial relationship needs
**#Ad**, in the caption, visible without tapping "more" — within the first
three lines on Instagram. In the US, the FTC requires the same disclosure at
the point of recommendation, with penalties running to five figures per
violation. This is the cheapest compliance win available and the easiest to
forget.

### TikTok — the primary channel

The product is inherently visual and the mechanism is a genuinely satisfying
reveal. Formats that suit it:

- **The snap.** Wet, wring, snap, then a thermometer or thermal reading on the
  fabric. This is the hero format and it should be remade endlessly.
- **Dry vs humid, side by side.** Two identical towels, two conditions, honest
  result. This is *our* format — the one competitors will not make because it
  shows the product's limit.
- **"You are using it wrong."** Not wringing hard enough is the single most
  common mistake. Correction videos travel.
- **Heat-safety explainers.** Signs of heat exhaustion, the buddy check.
  Genuinely useful, highly shareable, and it builds the authority the whole
  site rests on.
Post the mechanism, not the discount. The site's whole position is explanation,
and the social account should sound like the site.

### Instagram

Reels are the same content, recut. Carousels do something TikTok cannot: the
heat-index risk bands, the heat exhaustion vs heat stroke table, and the
wet-wring-snap sequence all work as saveable multi-panel posts. **Saves are the
metric that matters here**, not likes.

### Facebook

Different job. The groups are where the audience already is — UK construction
and trades groups, equestrian and livery groups, running and cycling clubs.
**Do not drop affiliate links into groups.** Answer the question properly, link
the guide when it genuinely helps, and let the site do the selling. Groups ban
link-droppers quickly and the ban is not appealable.

### The point of all three

Social does not need to convert directly. It needs to produce **branded
search** — people typing "cleanlivinghq heat calculator" — which is the
strongest signal a young domain can generate, and the one thing no competitor
can copy.

## Measurement

Set up on launch day, not later:

- Google Search Console on the **bare** domain, sitemap submitted
- GA4, via `GA_ID` — remember it also switches the consent banner and the
  policy wording
- The affiliate dashboard's sub-id report. `<AffiliateLink>` already stamps
  every outbound link with its source page, so this report is the one that
  tells you which content earns.

**Baseline: zero.** New domain, no history. Record the first 30 days after
`LAUNCHED` flips and compare against that, rather than re-deriving it later.

Watch: impressions before clicks, position before impressions. On a new domain
the first three months of clicks are noise, and reacting to them is how good
content gets deleted early.
